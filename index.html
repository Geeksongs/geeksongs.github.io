Hello, I'm Python Song.

I am |

I graduated with a master's degree from Columbia University. My research spans foundation models, world models, robot learning, AI for science, and AGI:

  • Foundation Models & World Models: In my first-author work, I led the development of the largest world model designed for Proactive AI, enabling agents to anticipate future states and proactively learn from interaction. This work was accepted as an EMNLP Oral (top ~1%).
  • Robot Learning: I developed a robot learning framework that evolves itself and achieves #1 performance on both LIBERO-Pro and RoboCasa365, surpassing results from the robotics company Physical Intelligence on two challenging benchmarks for robot generalization.
  • AI for Science: Recently, I have also been exploring AI-driven scientific discovery. I surpassed the previous world record on the three-color Pythagorean triples problem and produced a first proof for an open problem in number theory.
  • AGI: I am researching how to create digital life inside a computer: with no loss function, no ground-truth data, and no backpropagation, I use Darwinian evolution to create digital life with few-shot autonomous learning ability, opening a new route toward true AGI. The paper has been submitted to Nature.

I conducted research under the guidance of Prof. Jesse Thomason at Georgia Tech, and Prof. Junfeng Yang and Prof. Shilong Liu at Columbia. I have also conducted robot learning research with Research Scientists Siddharth Ancha at UC Berkeley and Ajay Mandlekar at NVIDIA.

I am actively seeking Ph.D. opportunities for Fall 2027.
I am particularly interested in foundation models, world models, robot learning, AI for science, and AGI, with a focus on building agents that learn proactively and evolve on their own.

    Simulation for Evaluating Robot Policies Should Be Asynchronous and Real-Time
    Python Song, Hyeonho Oh, Ishika Singh, Jesse Thomason, Ajay Mandlekar, and Siddharth Ancha
    IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2026
    Abs PDF Video Code Website

    Current robot simulation benchmarks run policies synchronously, giving unlimited thinking time per step – unrealistic for real-world deployment. We introduce AsyncRoboSim, which runs the policy and simulator in parallel processes communicating through shared observation/action queues, while the simulator keeps advancing in real time. This better reflects real inference latency and improves correlation between simulation and real-world performance.
